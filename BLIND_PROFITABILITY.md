# Is Playing the Blinds Profitable for You (If You Fix the Leaks)?

## Short answer
**No single blind seat is a *stand-alone* profit center for anyone — not even top
regs. The blinds are always net-negative seats. The real question is: "How much
less will they lose?" And for you the answer is: a LOT less — enough to flip your
overall game from a loser to a winner.** You don't "make money" in the blinds; you
*stop bleeding* in them, and the money you keep is your winrate.

---

## 1. Your ACTUAL blind results (this sample, 401 blind hands)

| Seat | Hands | VPIP | PFR | Net | Winrate |
|------|-------|------|-----|-----|---------|
| **SB** | 201 | 52.7% | 41.8% | −$348.8 (in bb) | **−173.5 bb/100** |
| **BB** | 200 | 46.5% | 9.0% | −$251.3 (in bb) | **−125.6 bb/100** |

> Context: a solid NL100 reg loses roughly **−30 to −45 bb/100 in the SB** and
> **−10 to −25 bb/100 in the BB**. These are *losses by design* — you're forced to
> post dead money. You are currently losing **4–5x the normal amount** in each blind.

**Interpretation:** Your blind losses are NOT variance. −173 bb/100 over 201 SB
hands is a structural strategy leak. The sample is big enough to trust the direction.

---

## 2. What "fixed" looks like — realistic targets

If you adopt the charts (SB raise-or-fold ~35% VPIP; BB defend ~42% VPIP but with
13–16% PFR and no sunk-cost barrels), realistic benchmarks are:

| Seat | Current | Target (fixed) | Improvement |
|------|---------|----------------|-------------|
| SB | −173.5 bb/100 | **−35 to −45 bb/100** | **≈ +130 bb/100** |
| BB | −125.6 bb/100 | **−15 to −25 bb/100** | **≈ +105 bb/100** |

These targets are *conservative* — they're the normal range for a competent (not
elite) reg. You will not get the SB to break even; nobody does.

---

## 3. What that does to your OVERALL winrate (the number that matters)

You play each seat ~1/6 of the time. Converting the per-seat improvement to your
**overall** winrate (improvement ÷ 6 per seat):

- SB fix: +130 bb/100 ÷ 6 ≈ **+21.7 bb/100 overall**
- BB fix: +105 bb/100 ÷ 6 ≈ **+17.5 bb/100 overall**
- **Combined blind fix ≈ +39 bb/100 to your TOTAL winrate.**

That is enormous. For scale, a strong NL100 reg's *entire* winrate is ~5–8 bb/100.
Your blind leaks alone are costing you roughly **5 full winrates'** worth of EV.

### Dollar translation at NL100 ($1 = 1bb), ~1,200 hands/session-batch like this one:
- Current blind bleed this sample: **≈ −$600** (SB −$349 + BB −$251, in bb terms).
- After the fix (conservative): blind bleed **≈ −$200** across the same volume.
- **≈ $400 saved per 1,200 hands** — purely from blind discipline, before any
  other leak fix (3-bet frequency, sunk-cost barrels).

---

## 4. The honest caveat (rake)

GGPoker Rush & Cash rake is heavy and **uncapped on small pots**, which hits blind
pots hardest (small, multiway, OOP). This is exactly why:
- You **cannot** play chip-EV-wide ranges from the blinds here — rake eats the
  thin spots.
- **Limping and loose flatting are especially bad** (you build small raked pots OOP).
- The rake is *also* why the SB can never be made profitable — don't chase that.
- **GG rakeback / Fish Buffet** recovers some of this; factor your rakeback % back
  in — it can be worth +3 to +6 bb/100 overall and effectively subsidizes marginal
  spots. Check your current rakeback tier.

---

## 5. Bottom line
- **Playing the blinds is "profitable" only in the sense of losing less.** That's
  true for everyone — the blinds are a damage-control seat, not a profit seat.
- **For YOU specifically: fixing blind play is the highest-ROI change available.**
  You're losing 4–5x the normal amount. Closing that gap adds an estimated
  **~+39 bb/100 to your overall winrate** and saves ~$400 per 1,200 hands at NL100.
- **Do not try to "win" in the SB/BB.** Try to *bleed slowly and correctly.* Fold
  more, raise-or-fold the SB, defend the BB wide-but-not-junk, add 3-bets, and
  stop barreling with no equity. The money you keep IS your winrate.
